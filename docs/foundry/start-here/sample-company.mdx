The sample company shows Foundry end to end on data you don't have to provide: Acme
Corp, a made-up company that sells scheduling software to field-service businesses.
Foundry connects its database, reads its documents, builds an ontology, loads it and
answers a question, one step after another on one page. Owners and admins can start it.

## Start the sample

On **Get started**, the page a workspace without an ontology opens on, select **See it
on a sample company (1 min)** under **Start with a sample company**. The page **Acme
Corp, a sample company** opens and runs each step by itself. Each step opens as its
work finishes and stays open, so the page reads top to bottom as what happened.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Acme Corp's data">
    Foundry connects Acme Corp's PostgreSQL database with a read-only login and syncs its
    tables: `accounts`, `contacts`, `subscriptions`, `invoices`, `support_tickets` and
    `product_usage`. It then uploads nine documents into a document set named **Acme
    Corp sample**: three customer contracts as PDFs, call notes, a support escalation,
    an incident report and a price list.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The goal">
    What the ontology is for, in Acme Corp's words: understanding each customer account
    with its people, plan, contract terms, invoices, tickets, usage and calls, to see
    which renewals are at risk.
  </Step>

  <Step title="The ontology">
    Foundry creates an ontology named **Acme Corp customers** on a new Helix database,
    from a ready-made draft, so there is no AI draft to wait for. It makes that version
    current and shows its types and the links between them.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Loading">
    The load writes Acme Corp's rows into the database as records, each linked to its
    account, subscription or contact, then reads the documents with AI models for the
    names and facts they hold. **See the load** opens its page.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ask the Analyst">
    Once the load is written, the page asks the ontology's first suggested question and
    shows the answer with the records and documents it came from. Under **Ask another**,
    pick another suggestion, which opens its thread in the
    [Analyst](/foundry/ask/ask-questions). **Ask your own question** opens a new
    thread, and **See the records** opens the [Explorer](/foundry/explore/explorer).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Now connect your own data">
    **Connect your own data** opens the first-ontology steps for your own sources, as in
    the [quickstart](/foundry/start-here/quickstart). **Set up with your agent** copies a
    prompt for your agent to do them with you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If a step fails, the page says why, with **Try again**. Reopening the page, or a reload,
carries on from where it was and makes nothing twice.

## While you explore the sample

While the sample is the workspace's only ontology, a banner across the top of every page
says **Sample data: you're exploring Acme Corp, a made-up company.** For owners and
admins, it links **Back to getting started**, or, once the sample's question is
answered, **Get started with your own data**.

The sample's connection and document set show in **Connectors** like any other, but
your own ontologies never offer them as sources. The ontology, its database and its
load are ordinary ones: the Analyst, the Explorer and Records read them as they read
yours.

## What it uses

- **A Helix database.** The sample creates one on Helix's shared cluster, in the
  workspace's first Helix project or a new project named after the workspace. It uses
  the workspace's Helix Cloud plan; see
  [Plans and billing](/foundry/workspace/plans-and-billing#the-helix-cloud-plan-for-databases).
- **AI models.** Reading the documents and answering questions use the workspace's
  [AI models](/foundry/workspace/ai-models), and count toward its
  [monthly caps](/foundry/workspace/usage) like any other load or question.

A day after you run the sample, if the workspace still has no data of its own, Foundry
sends you one email suggesting you try it on your own data.

To remove the sample, delete the **Acme Corp customers** ontology from its overview, then
delete its connection and documents in **Connectors**.
