Helix Foundry turns the tools your company runs on into one connected graph. Connect
sources such as Stripe or your PostgreSQL database, send it your app's or agent's own
data, upload the documents your tables don't cover, let Foundry's agent draft an
ontology across them, then load it into a Helix database, ask it questions, and
explore it as a graph or as records. Connectors keep syncing on a schedule, and
ontologies refresh when their data changes.

Foundry runs at [foundry.helix-db.com](https://foundry.helix-db.com), and your agents
reach it through the [Helix MCP](/foundry/agents/helix-mcp). Sign in with the account
you use for Helix Cloud; your workspaces come with it.

## How it fits together

| Concept | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Workspace** | Your team's space, shared with Helix Cloud. It holds connections, documents, ontologies, members and the Foundry plan. |
| **Connection** | A source Foundry reads, with its credential. Each **sync** copies its tables into a snapshot Foundry keeps, on the plan's schedule or when you ask. |
| **API source** | A source your apps and agents send pieces of data to, over HTTP or the Helix MCP. |
| **Document** | A file of the workspace, such as a contract, kept in Connectors. A load reads text and PDFs for the names and facts they hold, and table files such as CSV as tables. |
| **Document set** | A folder of documents. A version reads the sets its generation picked. |
| **Ontology** | The types, links and rules that describe your data, bound to one Helix database. |
| **Generation** | A request to Foundry's agent to draft a new version of the ontology from the tables, API sources and document sets you pick. |
| **Version** | One draft of the ontology. Versions never change; you choose which one is current. |
| **Load** | One write of the current version into the ontology's database: your tables, then the documents of its sets. A **refresh** is a load Foundry starts itself when the data changed. |
| **Ask** | Questions about a load in plain language, answered with the sources they came from. Foundry opens on it. |
| **Explorer** | The graph and records view of a load, read from the database with your session. |

The work flows in one direction:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  A[Connect data and upload documents] --> B[Generate a draft]
  B --> C[Review and make it current]
  C --> D[Load into the database]
  D --> E[Ask and explore]
  A -. syncs .-> D
```

You can do each step on the website, or have your agent do it through the
[Helix MCP](/foundry/agents/helix-mcp), with your confirmation for every change.

## What Foundry does not do

- It never writes to your sources. Connections only read, and database users need
  `SELECT` only.
- It never deletes a Helix database. Deleting an ontology removes what its loads wrote
  and leaves the database's other data in place.
- It does not manage roles, member removal or single sign-on. Those stay in the
  [Helix dashboard](https://helix-db.com/login).

## How Foundry uses AI models

A generation sends your synced tables, and up to five of the newest documents of each
set it picks, to Foundry's agent, which drafts the version. A load sends your
documents' text to AI models, which find the names and facts they hold and check them.
Reading tables into a load uses no models. Ask sends your question, and what each step
reads from the load, to AI models.

By default these run on hosted providers through Helix, today Anthropic's Claude models.
On Pro or Max, an owner can run them on the workspace's own Anthropic key instead; see
[AI models](/foundry/workspace/ai-models). What they cost counts against the
workspace's [monthly caps](/foundry/workspace/usage).
