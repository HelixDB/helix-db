A connection is a source Foundry reads, such as a Stripe account or a PostgreSQL
database. Each sync copies its tables into a snapshot that generations and loads read,
and Foundry syncs every connection on a schedule. Owners and admins add, sync, edit and
delete connections; members see them.

## The Connectors section

**Connectors**, in the sidebar, holds the workspace's connections,
[API sources](/foundry/data/api-sources) and [documents](/foundry/data/documents). The
Connectors explorer, a panel beside the sidebar, lists them as a tree; the page beside
it shows what you open.

**Connectors** itself is an overview: how many connections, tables, records, documents
and document sets the workspace has, a grid of its connections with their status,
tables, records and last sync, and its document sets with the ontologies that read each
one. Select a connection's row to open it.

## The Connectors explorer

The explorer's tree has, at its root:

- a folder per kind of connection the workspace has, such as **Stripe** or
  **PostgreSQL**, holding those connections;
- folders of your own, each holding the connections moved into it;
- **Documents**, with a folder per document set holding its documents.

Select a connection to open its page, or a document to open its page. A connection's dot
shows its last sync's status, and its arrow opens its synced tables. **Filter** narrows
the tree to what matches, tables included. The row of the page you have open is
highlighted, and the folders holding it open.

The tree works from the keyboard: the arrow keys move between rows and open and close
folders, <kbd>Home</kbd> and <kbd>End</kbd> go to the first and last rows,
<kbd>Enter</kbd> opens a row, and <kbd>F2</kbd> renames it.

## Arrange the explorer

Owners and admins arrange the tree for the whole workspace; everyone sees the same
order and names, and members can't change them.

- **Rename** a folder, connection, document set or document with the pencil beside its
  name, or <kbd>F2</kbd> on its row. <kbd>Enter</kbd> saves and <kbd>Escape</kbd>
  cancels. Renaming a kind's folder or Documents changes only the folder's name.
- **Reorder** a row by dragging its grip, or select the grip for **Move up** and **Move
  down**. A connection's grip also has **Move to…**, to move it into one of your
  folders or back to its kind's, and a document's has **Move to another set…**.
- **New folder**, in the explorer's head, makes a folder of connections at the root.
  Drag connections into it, or move them from their menus. Folders hold connections
  only, and don't nest.
- **Delete folder**, from a folder's grip, sends its connections back to their kinds'
  folders. Nothing else changes.

<Frame>
  <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/data/explorer-menu.png" alt="The Connectors explorer with a connection's grip menu open: Move up, Move down and Move to…" />
</Frame>

Rows can't be moved while a filter is on. Sync, and a document set's upload, describe and
delete buttons, show on a row while you point at it or it has the focus.

## Add a connection

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the source">
    Select **Add data** on the Connectors overview, or **+** in the explorer's head, then
    choose where the data is: an app, a database, files in Amazon S3, or an
    [API source](/foundry/data/api-sources) your app or agent sends data to. A tick marks
    a kind the workspace already connects; you can add another. Every plan offers every
    kind.

    <Frame>
      <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/data/add-data.png" alt="The Where is your data? picker with Stripe, WorkOS, PostHog, GitHub, Linear, Cal.com and Granola, then PostgreSQL and MySQL, Amazon S3, and API source" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create a read-only credential">
    Follow the numbered steps above the form. They link to the place in the source
    where you create the key or user, and say which read permissions to give it.
    [Connectors](/foundry/data/connectors) lists them for every source.

    <Frame>
      <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/data/stripe-form.png" alt="The Stripe form with its three numbered steps above the Restricted API key field" />
    </Frame>

    Under **Connection options** you can name the connection; databases also choose
    their encryption there.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Test the connection">
    Select **Test connection**. Foundry reads the source with the credential and lists
    what it can read: each table with its key and its links to other tables. Anything
    the credential can't read is named below the list and is skipped. For a database or
    S3, choose the tables to sync here. Every table starts selected, and keeping them
    all also syncs tables added later; at least one must stay selected.

    <Frame>
      <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/data/stripe-tested.png" alt="A successful Stripe test listing the objects the key can read with their keys and links" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Connect and sync">
    Select **Connect and sync**. Foundry stores the credential encrypted, opens the
    connection's page and queues its first sync. The connection joins its kind's folder
    in the explorer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

An agent connected through the [Helix MCP](/foundry/agents/helix-mcp) can't add a
connection itself: it gives you a link that opens this form for the kind you asked for,
so credentials never pass through the agent.

## Follow a sync

A connection's page shows its status, its recent syncs and its tables. A sync is
**Queued**, then **Syncing**, then **Synced** or **Failed**; the page follows it as it
runs. An attempt that fails for a passing reason runs again a minute or two later, shown
as **Retrying**, up to three attempts. When a sync fails, the page says why above its
history.

<Frame>
  <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/data/connection.png" alt="A synced Stripe connection with its recent syncs, details and table cards" />
</Frame>

A table that finished keeps its new snapshot even if a later table fails. A connection
runs one sync at a time. While one runs, its settings can't change and it can't be
deleted.

## Syncs on a schedule

Foundry syncs every connection on its own, as often as the workspace's
[plan](/foundry/workspace/plans-and-billing) says:

| Plan | Syncs | Each sync reads |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | Once a day | Every table in full |
| Pro | Every hour | Only what changed since the last sync, and every table in full once a day |
| Max | Every 15 minutes | Only what changed since the last sync, and every table in full once a day |

Reading only what changed uses what each source can tell: Stripe's and WorkOS's events,
records updated since the last sync, or new files in S3. A daily full read catches what
those can't see, such as deleted records. PostgreSQL and MySQL tables, and any table
without a primary key, are read in full each time and compared with the last snapshot.
A Stripe key needs **Read** on Events for this; without it, every sync reads every table
in full, and the test says so.

You can also sync at any time: select **Sync now** on a connection's page, or **Sync**
on its row in the explorer. A connection also syncs after you change its settings. An
[API source](/foundry/data/api-sources) syncs about a minute after pieces arrive, on
every plan.

When a sync changes tables an ontology's current load reads, the ontology
[refreshes](/foundry/ontologies/load-data#automatic-refreshes) by itself.

## Read a synced table

Select a table to see its columns, its primary key, the columns that point at other
tables, the columns in other tables that point at it, and when its snapshot was taken.
An app's columns are the fields found in its rows, without types.

## Edit or delete a connection

Open the **⋯** menu on a connection's page:

- **Edit connection** changes its settings or credential. Foundry tests the new
  settings before it saves them, and syncs again when they changed. Leave the
  credential blank to keep the stored one. To change which database tables sync, enter
  the password and test the connection again.
- **Delete connection** stops syncing it and removes its tables from the workspace's
  data. The source itself is not changed.

Foundry never shows a stored credential again. An API key is named by its prefix and
last four characters, such as `rk_live…Qx7m`; a database shows that a password is set.
