By default, every member of a workspace sees everything in it. Under **Settings** →
**Access**, owners and admins can share sources instead. A member, a team or an agent's
key then reads only what those sources hold of each ontology.

## What you can share

| Source | Shares |
| --- | --- |
| A connection | Every table it syncs, now and later, and the document sets it writes |
| A table | One table of a connection |
| A document set | Its documents |
| People enrichment | The profiles a load looked up |

Sharing works on each ontology's current load: the entities, facts and documents that
those sources filled. Someone who holds every source a load reads reads the whole
ontology. Someone who holds some of them reads only that part.

## Give someone sources

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open their sources">
    In **Settings** → **Access**, open **Agents**, **People** or **Teams**, then select
    **Sources** on a row.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the sources">
    Tick connections, tables, document sets or People enrichment. Ticking a connection
    includes its tables and sets.

    As you choose, **Can read** lists each ontology they'll read: all of it, or
    **Part of it**, which names the sources they're missing. **Can't read yet** lists the
    other ontologies, each with the sources it reads.

    <Frame>
      <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/workspace/access-sources.png" alt="The Sources dialog for a member: the General set ticked, and Customer contracts listed as part of it under Can read" />
    </Frame>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Select **Save**. Giving a member or team their first sources restricts them, so
    Foundry asks first: select **Save and restrict**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

**Load again to share** beside an ontology means its current load can't be shared,
because it was loaded before Foundry kept track of what each load reads. Load it again.

## Restricted members

Giving a member sources restricts them. A restricted member:

- sees only **Analyst**, **Ontologies** and **Explorer** in the sidebar: no
  Connectors, Settings or search, and only the theme and **Sign out** in the account
  menu;
- sees only the ontologies their sources cover, and only what those sources hold of them;
- asks the Analyst and reads records, entities and documents, but changes nothing;
- has no graph: the Explorer opens **Records**, and an ontology's pages other than its
  overview, such as its loads and versions, open the overview.

A restricted member who holds no sources yet sees **Nothing shared with you yet**.

Where they hold only part of an ontology, its pages say so.

<Frame>
  <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/workspace/access-restricted.png" alt="A restricted member's view of an ontology they can read part of, with only Analyst, Ontologies and the Explorer in the sidebar" />
</Frame>

Taking sources away doesn't lift the restriction. Without them, the member reads less,
or nothing. To lift it, take away every source they hold, their own and their teams',
then turn off **Restricted** for them under **People**.

<Frame>
  <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/workspace/access-people.png" alt="The People tab: the owner, a member who can't be restricted because of raw access, and a restricted member who reads part of an ontology" />
</Frame>

Some people can't be restricted:

- **Owners and admins** always see everything.
- **Members with raw access** can't be restricted. Raw access means the member writes to
  a project or queries its databases outside Foundry, which is set in the
  [Helix dashboard](https://helix-db.com/login). Take that access away there first. A
  team that has such a member can't be given sources either. If a restricted member is
  given raw access later, they're flagged **Raw access**, because Foundry can't limit
  what they do outside it.

## Teams

Under **Teams**, select **New team** and choose its name and members. Then give the team
sources once, and every member reads what those cover.

Once the team has sources, its members are restricted, and they stay restricted after
leaving it. Deleting a team takes away what it gave: its members keep only what they
hold otherwise.

## Agent keys

An agent's key reads and asks only the ontologies its sources cover, through the
[Helix MCP](/foundry/agents/helix-mcp). It changes nothing.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make a key">
    Under **Agents**, select **New key**, name it and select **Create key**. Copy the key
    now: Foundry shows it once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give it sources">
    Select **Sources** on its row and choose what it reads, as for a person.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use it">
    Give the key to the agent as its MCP bearer token, as in
    [Service credentials](/foundry/agents/helix-mcp#service-credentials).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame>
  <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/workspace/access-agents.png" alt="The Agents tab: an agent key and the ontology its sources let it read" />
</Frame>

**Revoke** stops the key at once, and its sources go with it. A key granted **Query read**
on a project in the Helix dashboard reads that project's ontologies whole, whatever its
sources.

## See who reads what

**Ontologies** lists each ontology's current load, who it is shared with, and who reads
only part of it.

<Frame>
  <img src="https://assets.helix-db.com/docs/foundry/workspace/access-ontologies.png" alt="The Ontologies tab: an ontology shared with an agent key, and in part with a member" />
</Frame>

Some changes would cut someone off from what they read: loading, making a version or a
load current, deleting a connection, or moving a document. Before any of these, Foundry
lists who would stop reading what, and asks first.

## Limits

Someone reading through sources asks up to 10 questions an hour and spends up to $10 a
month on questions. Their questions also count toward the workspace's own
[limits](/foundry/reference/limits#analyst).

Reading through sources also shows less than full access does:

- Documents open as uploaded, without highlights.
- Entity pages have no mentions or reviews.
- A type they hold only part of shows its first records, without pages, and can't be
  filtered or sorted by what they hold only part of.
